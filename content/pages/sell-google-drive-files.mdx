## A real limitation: large files and Google's 25MB threshold

Files over 25MB sometimes prompt buyers to sign into a Google account before downloading, depending on their browser and Google's bandwidth throttling. In practice, most buyers have Google accounts, so this rarely causes friction.

For very large files — video courses, full asset libraries, files over 100MB — Dropbox direct download links are more reliable for anonymous download. The workflow on unseal.link is identical; just paste a Dropbox shared link instead of a Drive link.

## Privacy considerations

When you share a Google Drive file as "anyone with the link," anyone who has that link can access it. The paywall controls who receives the link, but once someone has it, they can share it.

For most creators, this is acceptable. Template buyers sharing the template with one friend is a minor edge case that does not meaningfully affect revenue.

For higher-value or more sensitive files:
- Create a version of the template for paid buyers (a separate copy) and change the sharing URL after each sale
- Use a more controlled access method (like a private GitHub repo where you add buyers manually)

A practical middle ground: rotate your Drive link periodically — generate a new shareable link, update your unseal.link product, and the old link stops working. This invalidates any shared copies without affecting your files. For products over $100, a quarterly rotation is reasonable.
